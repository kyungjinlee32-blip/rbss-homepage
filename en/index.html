SVC-01 · PSM

PSM Report Preparation & Approval

We handle the whole submission: accurate, systematic collection of process safety information, industry-specific procedures for all 12 PSM elements, permitting engineering such as hazardous area classification drawings, and training for the implementation assessment.

SVC-02 · HAZOP

HAZOP Studies

We run HAZOP on P&IDs verified against the field, identify process hazards and recommend practical, actionable safeguards.

SVC-03 · QRA

Consequence Analysis & QRA

We model worst-case and alternative release scenarios with tools such as PHAST, and feed the results into emergency response plan revisions and plant layout reviews.

SVC-04 · LOPA

Layer of Protection Analysis (LOPA)

We evaluate independent protection layers (IPLs) for high-risk HAZOP scenarios and identify additional safeguards needed to meet the target risk level.

SVC-05 · AUDIT

PSM Assessment & Internal Audit

We assess PSM implementation and conduct internal audits, moving sites from paper compliance to practical operation of each element.

SVC-06 · CULTURE

Safety Culture Assessment

Using international standards such as ISRS, we assess safety culture and safety management system maturity and build an improvement roadmap.

SVC-07 · SAPA

Serious Accidents Punishment Act Compliance

Through safety and health management system reviews, assessments and audits, we verify how top management is meeting its legal duties and set out the gaps to close.

SVC-08 · OUTSOURCE

Outsourced Safety Manager Services

As a designated safety management agency, we take on the duties of the safety manager and the safety & health officer and carry out your site's statutory safety management.

SVC-09 · EDU

Training & Learning Teams

Hands-on training for everyone from PSM beginners to practitioners, and a Learning Team Program where frontline workers learn and improve together.

05Q&A

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about process safety management in Korea and RBSS services. Ask us anything using the form.

Q1Does our site have to submit a Process Safety Management (PSM) report?

In Korea, a PSM report is required if your site is in a designated industry such as crude oil refining, petrochemicals, chemical fertilizers or explosives, or if it manufactures, handles or stores designated hazardous substances above threshold quantities (Occupational Safety and Health Act Article 44, Enforcement Decree Article 43).

Inventories of different substances must be combined to decide, so send us your list of substances and maximum inventories and we will review applicability with you.

Q2When is the PSM report due, and how long does it take to prepare?

When you install, relocate or make major changes to hazardous facilities, the report must be submitted to KOSHA at least 30 days before construction starts.

Preparation usually takes several months, depending on process size and the state of existing documentation. Process safety information, hazard analysis (HAZOP etc.), safe operating plans and the emergency response plan are prepared in sequence, so we recommend starting early.

Q3How can we raise our PSM implementation assessment grade (P, S, M+, M-)?

The grade reflects whether the 12 elements actually work in the field, not just on paper. Start with areas that are often cited in assessments, such as management of change (MOC), inspection and maintenance, work permits and training records.

RBSS finds the gaps through internal audits aligned with the assessment criteria, then supports practitioner training and improvement planning.

Q4Is a HAZOP or other risk assessment a one-time exercise?

No. When equipment or operating conditions change, the risk assessment must be redone under the management of change procedure, and it should be revalidated after incidents or near misses and at regular intervals to stay current.

We select the right technique for your process, such as HAZOP, Checklist, What-if or LOPA.

Q5Where should we start with the Serious Accidents Punishment Act?

The starting point is a safety and health management system: safety and health goals and policy set by top management, procedures to identify and control hazards, adequate budget and staff, semi-annual reviews, and records that prove implementation.

RBSS assesses your current system and sets out the gaps and priorities.

Q6Can we outsource the safety manager role?

Sites with fewer than 300 regular employees can entrust safety manager duties to a safety management agency designated by the Ministry of Employment and Labor (some industries excluded). Safety and health officer duties at smaller sites can also be outsourced.

As a designated agency, RBSS provides regular site visits, risk assessments, training and record keeping. Tell us your industry and headcount and we will confirm eligibility.

Q7How does an engagement work, and how is the fee set?

Consultation, then a site visit and document review, then a proposal on scope, schedule and fee, then delivery and follow-up support.

Fees depend on process size, scope and existing documentation, so we quote after the site visit. The first consultation carries no obligation.

Q8Where can you deliver training and site visits?

From our Suncheon head office and Songdo (Incheon) office we visit sites anywhere in Korea, and we work on international projects. Safety culture training, practical PSM training and the Learning Team Program can be held on site to suit your schedule, in Korean or English.

Regulations may change. We confirm how they apply based on the latest law and your site conditions.
